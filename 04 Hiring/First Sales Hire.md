# First Sales Hire

## When To Hire

Do not hire the first salesperson to discover the market for you. Hire after the founder has learned enough to describe the wedge, buyer, repeatable pain, and early buying motion.

## What To Look For

Jen's shorthand: the person needs to be able to "cosplay" the founder.

That means:

- Carrying vision without sounding scripted.
- Pattern-matching messy buyer context.
- Asking direct questions.
- Building trust quickly.
- Improvising narrative and frame.
- Understanding enterprise buying dynamics.
- Staying close to product and founder learning.

## Non-Traditional Sales Talent

Jen repeatedly argues that non-sales talent can make excellent sales talent. The point is not to avoid sales skill; it is to avoid overly polished behavior that makes buyers feel sold to.

## Logo Heuristic

Be careful when hiring from prestigious sales logos. A strong brand, marketing engine, and mature category may have done much of the selling. For early startup sales, a rep from a less glamorous logo may have learned more scrappy, founder-like selling.

## Stage Fit

Early stages need generalists who can operate in ambiguity. Later stages need specialists. A small-business seller dropped into enterprise is risky, and a late-stage enterprise seller dropped into messy zero-to-one work can also fail.
