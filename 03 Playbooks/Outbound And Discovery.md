# Outbound And Discovery

## Goal

Create a conversation where the buyer feels the founder understands something important about their world, then use the conversation to learn whether the pain, urgency, and path to action are real.

## Outreach Principles

- Write manually before buying tools or automating.
- Keep the note short — **2–3 sentences max** to an executive.
- Make the message relevant to the prospect's role.
- Lead with a counterintuitive or specific problem insight.
- Avoid sounding like a polished sales sequence.
- Do not just say the product is better. Show why the lens is different.
- Lead with **alpha** — the unfair advantage they unlock (rethink the business model / restructure the team), not "we do X 10x better." Litmus test: what would they say about the product in a board meeting? Iterate the message over time.

## Two Entry Layers And The Pincer Model

Enter through **only two layers**: the executive (e.g. chief legal officer / GC) or the **N-minus-one** (a VP/director one step removed). Go any lower and the storyline degrades into a game of telephone — you learn user value, not executive value, and a $100K deal needs an executive sponsor to allocate budget.

Use the **pincer model**: the founder targets the executive; the AE targets the N-minus-one; you work to bring the two together. Executives respond one-to-one (email, LinkedIn, cold call, event, Twitter), not to marketing campaigns — and the best sender to an executive is the founder. Cold calls work for non-technical buyers, not technical ones.

## First Call

Do not rush into a demo. The first call should gather context:

- What is happening internally?
- Why is this urgent now?
- Who cares most?
- Who owns the pain?
- Who would block or slow it?
- What language do they already use for the problem?
- What would make the buyer look smart internally?

## Buyer-Led Demo Trick

When there is enough trust, ask the buyer to show how they currently do the workflow before you show your product. This reveals pain, language, context, and the exact moments where your demo should land.

## The Intro Call Is The Most Important Call

Buyers are at their most open on the first call and clam up later once it feels like a process — so all the information edge you'll ever get is here. Keep it informal, ~30 minutes, **no demo, no slides, and no recorder** (recording kills vulnerability). Open low-pressure and **let them talk first**; spend only the back ~10 minutes on what you do, framed around what they just shared. No scripted pitch — BANT stays in the back of your mind and is never asked directly. Full step-by-step in [Full Enterprise Sales Cycle](<Full Enterprise Sales Cycle.md>).

## Next Step Discipline

Try to book the next call while still live. A vague "send me something" is often a polite exit unless it is tied to a specific internal job the buyer is doing.
