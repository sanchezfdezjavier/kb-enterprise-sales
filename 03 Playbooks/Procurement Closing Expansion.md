# Procurement Closing Expansion

## Procurement Is A Process To Manage

Enterprise procurement is not just paperwork at the end. It is part of the buyer's internal categorization and risk process. The seller's job is to remove friction, anticipate objections, and help the buyer carry the deal through.

## Practical Moves

- Learn whose paper will be used.
- Expect the enterprise's MSA or SOW to matter.
- Help the buyer understand how procurement will classify the vendor.
- Fill out forms quickly.
- Answer security and legal questions with clarity.
- Keep the internal champion equipped with concise language.

## Papering Mechanics

- Document the reverse-engineered timeline in an email the champion can forward to procurement: agreed pricing **predicated on signing by date X**, plus a **kicker** (e.g. a free month) to create urgency.
- Send **your paper as a Word doc, never a PDF** (they will redline) — most enterprises use Microsoft Word, not Google Docs. Offer: use ours, or lift into theirs (theirs may be faster to their timeline).
- Redlines are guaranteed. Accept the easy ones; when extensive, get **legal on a live call** rather than endless async back-and-forth. Focus on terms that impact the business; let minor stuff go. Have your own counsel review theirs.
- If it's *their* paper, expect a kitchen-sink first draft (e.g. $10M liability insurance) — it's all negotiable, but **pick your battles**. The deal will not fall apart over reasonable pushback at this stage.
- **Confirm the signatory** before routing (sometimes the CFO, not the sponsor) so delays get pinged to the right person.

## Procurement Is The Only Path To Payment

Procurement's job is **not to kill the deal** — it's to ensure the buy fits how they purchase (~30-day process). But it is the only route to getting paid: business-unit leaders can't pay you; finance pulls from budget. **Do no implementation or onboarding work until the papers are signed.** Note: "stuck in procurement" is also a common excuse buyers use to avoid delivering bad news — read it against your champion's signal.

## Co-Author The Buying Story

The close depends on the buyer's internal story. Co-author:

- Why change.
- Why now.
- Why this vendor.
- Why this budget.
- Why this scope.

This is more important than a generic ROI slide.

## Expansion

Enterprise sales has always been about expansion. Landing is only the first motion. After land, project-manage the use case, earn preferred-vendor status, and use insider context to find the next business unit or owner.

## Two Layers Of PMF

Jen points to two layers in enterprise:

- Land: product-market fit with the buyer.
- Expand: product-market fit with the user.

Flat enterprise accounts are a warning sign. A real enterprise motion should create a path to larger annual value over time. For the first ~10 enterprise customers, keep the founder involved after landing to learn what's custom to each buyer and whether to build it out or sell it as services. **Services are a legitimate line item** — it's what enterprises already know how to buy.
