# Qualification Signals

## Strong Signals

- There is internal pressure or incentive to solve the problem.
- You have cell or text access, not only calendar access.
- The buyer shares insider context, constraints, or internal dynamics.
- The buyer helps shape the internal narrative.
- The buyer is willing to be direct and specific.
- The buyer can explain where budget will come from.
- The buyer lets you understand who must defend, approve, block, or use the purchase.

## Weak Or Misleading Signals

- "Circle back."
- A request for generic collateral.
- A request for a case study with no concrete buying context.
- A group demo before real discovery.
- Multi-level communication before one committed owner exists.
- A buyer who only asks for pricing before explaining funding and defense.
- A call recorder in a sensitive enterprise conversation.
- Vague excitement with no next step or internal role.

## Practical Test

Ask: "What have I learned that my competitors do not know?"

If the answer is nothing, the deal is probably under-qualified. Jen's view of enterprise selling rewards the team that collects the most useful internal context.

## Qualify On Change, Not Problem

Everyone speaks to the same *problem*, which commoditizes you. Qualify instead on **change**: does the buyer admit something must change, is it dictated top-down, and why now rather than a year from now? Roughly **1 in 4 intro calls should disqualify** — usually on maturity, not on talking to the wrong person ("the gap is too big; let's revisit in a year"). You do not have to nail every call.

## Win-Rate Benchmarks

- Healthy enterprise **win rate is ~25–35%** of qualified opportunities. If it is higher, **your price is too low** — part of the market simply isn't mature enough, and that's fine.
- Rough funnel: ~50–75% of well-targeted outreach → qualified lead; ~half of those → demo; ~a quarter advance beyond. Bottom of funnel loses little if qualification was good.
- **Pilot success ~80% (only ~20% drop-off).** Below that, the pilot or qualification is off — or you are over-convincing on a product that isn't ready.
- ~25% of *lost* deals boomerang within a year — a "no" is often just timing (maturity, acquisition, frozen vendor intake).
