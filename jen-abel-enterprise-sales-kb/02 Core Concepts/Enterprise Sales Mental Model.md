# Enterprise Sales Mental Model

## Summary

Jen's enterprise sales model is less about polished selling and more about buyer-side context. The job is to understand the internal pressure, gather insider information, shape a narrative the buyer can retell, and project-manage the buying process to a decision.

## What Enterprise Sales Is

- Reading between the lines.
- Getting off-the-record context.
- Learning the existing corporate narrative.
- Co-authoring the story with the buyer.
- Helping the buyer defend the purchase internally.
- Project-managing the buyer's process.

## What Enterprise Sales Is Not

- A generic group demo too early.
- A case study request treated as strong intent.
- A deck or one-pager as the main selling surface.
- A polished sales motion that avoids direct questions.
- A pilot in the middle of the funnel.

## Buyer Story

The buyer needs language for:

- Why change.
- Why now.
- Why this team.
- Why this vendor.
- Why this budget deserves priority.

If the startup cannot help write that story, the buyer has to carry too much internal load alone.

## Enterprise Is A Different Game

Jen repeatedly frames enterprise as offline, off-script, and often off the record. That does not mean sloppy. It means the real sale often happens through trust, cell-phone access, context gathering, and highly specific internal language.

## Alpha Is The Engine

The organizing idea across the full cycle is **alpha** — the information edge you collect from insiders that competitors never get. You are always up against *something*: the status quo, a competitor, or (most often) an incumbent. Every extra call (follow-up, pre-demo, post-demo debrief, post-pilot) exists to widen that edge so the buyer feels the product and the process were built specifically for them.

You sell alpha to the executive too: not "we reduce time on task X" but the unfair advantage they unlock — how they rethink the business model or restructure the team. If you cannot excite an executive, you are not ready to run a top-down enterprise motion.

## Mirror The Buying Process, Not A CRM Pipeline

The traditional five stages (intro, demo, proposal, contracting, closed won/lost) are a **weighted-forecast tool**, not a sales process. Each stage is really a bucket of three-to-five critical meetings. The real motion is closer to 15 steps, and ~90% of sellers get it wrong by plopping buyers into their five-step process instead of mirroring the buyer's own buying process.
