# Jen Abel Enterprise Sales Map

## 1. Founder-Led Sales Is The Learning Loop

The founder should sell early because the sale is where product, market, positioning, and trust collide.

See: [Founder-Led Sales](<../02 Core Concepts/Founder-Led Sales.md>)

## 2. Enterprise Sales Is Context Collection

The seller's advantage comes from insider context, not generic persuasion.

See: [Enterprise Sales Mental Model](<../02 Core Concepts/Enterprise Sales Mental Model.md>)

## 3. Qualification Is About Access And Pressure

Strong deals show cell access, internal pressure, buyer-owned language, and off-the-record context. Weak deals hide behind generic collateral requests, case studies, or vague excitement.

See: [Qualification Signals](<../02 Core Concepts/Qualification Signals.md>)

## 4. The Real Cycle Is ~15 Steps, Not 5

The CRM five-stage pipeline is a weighted-forecast tool, not a sales process. The real motion is closer to 15 steps — and the most decisive ones (follow-up call, pre-demo, post-demo debrief, pilot move-forward, post-pilot session) are the ones most sellers skip. Mirror the buyer's process; don't plop them into yours.

See: [Full Enterprise Sales Cycle](<../03 Playbooks/Full Enterprise Sales Cycle.md>)

## 5. Discovery Comes Before Demo

The first calls should expose the buyer's current workflow, constraints, urgency, and internal story. A generic demo too early weakens the deal.

See: [Outbound And Discovery](<../03 Playbooks/Outbound And Discovery.md>)

## 6. Pilots And Pricing Are Late

Pilots should validate a deal that is already real. Pricing should wait until the seller understands funding, defense, and internal category.

See: [Demos Pilots Pricing](<../03 Playbooks/Demos Pilots Pricing.md>)

## 7. Closing Is Project Management

Procurement, legal, security, and internal approvals must be managed with the buyer. The seller helps write the story the buyer will retell.

See: [Procurement Closing Expansion](<../03 Playbooks/Procurement Closing Expansion.md>)

## 8. Design Partners And Services Are Tools, Not Strategy By Default

Use design partners and services to learn and enter, but time-box them and connect them to a real buying path.

See: [Design Partners Services](<../03 Playbooks/Design Partners Services.md>)

## 9. The First Sales Hire Must Preserve Founder Energy

The first sales hire should carry vision, ask direct questions, and pattern-match enterprise context before the company over-specializes.

See: [First Sales Hire](<../04 Hiring/First Sales Hire.md>)
